Arduino-Based Posture Correction
Device Using MPU6050

As part of an IDEA Lab project at TKM College of Engineering, I worked on developing a smart posture-correcting wearable system aimed at addressing the growing issue of poor posture caused by prolonged sitting and device usage. The project focused on designing a compact and user-friendly belt integrated with an Arduino-based system and an MPU6050 motion sensor to continuously monitor spinal alignment in real time.

The system was designed to detect deviations from a calibrated neutral posture and trigger a vibration alert only when incorrect posture was sustained beyond a threshold duration, ensuring accuracy while avoiding false alarms. I was actively involved in system integration, circuit design, and implementation, including working with components such as sensors, microcontrollers, and feedback mechanisms. The entire setup was embedded into a wearable belt to ensure comfort, portability, and practical usability in everyday scenarios.

This project provided hands-on experience in combining mechanical design with embedded systems, while also addressing a real-world health problem through an affordable and scalable solution. It strengthened my understanding of sensor-based systems, user-centric design, and interdisciplinary engineering approaches.

Material Optimization for
Coconut Husk Peeler

Replacing Mild Steel with SS316 for Improved Strength & Durability

As part of a materials engineering project at TKM College of Engineering, I worked on evaluating and improving the performance of coconut husk peeler equipment by replacing conventional Mild Steel components with Stainless Steel (SS316). The project addressed a real-world issue where Mild Steel components frequently fail due to bending, wear, and corrosion under continuous mechanical loading and humid operating conditions.

To analyze this, we conducted a comparative experimental study between Mild Steel and SS316, focusing on mechanical properties and environmental resistance. I was involved in material analysis and testing processes including microstructure examination, hardness testing, tensile testing using a Universal Testing Machine (UTM), and corrosion testing through saltwater exposure. The results showed that SS316 exhibited higher hardness (~86.6 BHN vs ~69.2 BHN), improved yield strength, and significantly better resistance to corrosion, with over 100 times lower corrosion rate compared to Mild Steel.

Through this study, we demonstrated that replacing Mild Steel with SS316 can effectively reduce deformation, minimize maintenance, and extend the operational lifespan of the equipment. This project enhanced my understanding of material selection, failure analysis, and the practical application of mechanical properties in real-world industrial systems.

CFD & Thermal Analysis of
Aerospike and Bell Nozzles

As part of a thermal engineering project at TKM College of Engineering, I conducted a comparative analysis of Aerospike and Bell nozzles to evaluate their aerodynamic performance, thermal behavior, and structural response under identical operating conditions. The study aimed to understand how nozzle geometry influences flow expansion, thrust efficiency, and heat distribution in rocket propulsion systems.

Both nozzles were designed using SolidWorks and analyzed using ANSYS Fluent, with boundary conditions including a chamber pressure of ~6.14 MPa, temperature of ~3006 K, and medium-altitude exit pressure. The CFD results provided detailed insights into Mach number, velocity, pressure, and temperature distributions, showing supersonic flow acceleration (up to Mach ~3.2 in the Bell nozzle) and efficient plume expansion in the Aerospike configuration.

Thermal and structural analysis using ANSYS revealed that while the Aerospike nozzle offers superior altitude compensation and aerodynamic efficiency, it experiences higher thermal loads and strain, particularly along the spike region. The study also identified critical cooling zones, emphasizing the need for regenerative cooling systems, especially at the throat and inner walls of the Bell nozzle and the spike region of the Aerospike nozzle.

This project enhanced my understanding of CFD, heat transfer, and simulation-driven design, while highlighting the trade-offs between performance efficiency and thermal management in aerospace systems.
